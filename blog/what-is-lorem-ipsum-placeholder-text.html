What Is Lorem Ipsum Placeholder Text? Simple Guide

📅 2026-06-15⏱ 8 min read✍ ToolsNowPro TeamUpdated 2026-07-23

Lorem Ipsum is placeholder text used to fill a design when the final copy is not ready yet. It gives a realistic shape to paragraphs without drawing attention to the actual words — which is exactly the point. The moment placeholder text says something interesting, people start reading it instead of judging the layout around it.

The passage itself comes from a scrambled section of Cicero's De Finibus Bonorum et Malorum, a Latin text from 45 BC. It was adopted by printers in the 1500s because the Latin roots looked like real language without forming actual words a reader would get distracted by, and it has stayed the industry default ever since.

Why designers still use it

Placeholder text lets you judge how a page will look once real content is added, without waiting for that content to exist. It is useful for testing line length, spacing, alignment, and the overall rhythm of a page — the things that are hard to evaluate on an empty wireframe with boxes labeled "text goes here."

That makes it a simple but important step for anyone building a mockup quickly: you can settle the structure first and let the words follow later, instead of blocking the whole design process on a finished draft of copy.

Lorem Ipsum vs. "dummy text" — is there a difference?

People often use the two terms interchangeably, but there is a small practical distinction. Lorem Ipsum specifically refers to the classic scrambled-Latin passage — the one every designer recognizes on sight. Dummy text is the broader category: any placeholder copy, which could be Lorem Ipsum, a repeated word, or custom filler written to loosely match your subject matter.

Use classic Lorem Ipsum when you want something instantly familiar that won't distract stakeholders during an early review. Reach for custom dummy text when you want the placeholder to hint at tone or length specific to your project — for example, writing filler that reads like product descriptions if that's what the section will eventually hold. Many teams start a project with generic Lorem Ipsum for the first layout pass, then switch to project-specific dummy copy once the structure is approved and the review shifts from "does this look right" to "does this message land."

Where it fits — and where it doesn't

Lorem Ipsum works best in early-stage design: landing pages, blog templates, dashboard cards, pricing tables, and email previews, anywhere the goal is a visual review rather than a content review. If a stakeholder is checking whether a section feels too cramped, too wide, or too dense, placeholder text gives them a realistic shape to react to without the distraction of unfinished messaging.

It is a poor fit, though, for anything close to publication, and for any page where the actual wording is what's being reviewed — legal pages, pricing copy, support instructions, product descriptions. In those cases the real text needs to be in place before anyone can meaningfully sign off on the page.

Using it for wireframes specifically

Wireframes are a slightly different case from polished mockups: the goal isn't a realistic visual, it's testing spacing, hierarchy, and how quickly a layout comes together. For a hero section, keep the placeholder short — a headline and one line is usually enough to check proportions. For a content block or blog preview, use two or three paragraphs so the layout behaves the way it will once real copy lands, since a wireframe with one sentence of filler in a spot meant for 200 words will hide layout problems that only show up once the block is full.

Choosing the right length for the block

The most common mistake is placeholder text that is too short: a block with only a few words tells you nothing about line wrapping or spacing pressure. The second most common mistake is the opposite — a block so long it makes the design look heavier than the real content ever will. Match the length to what the section will actually hold: one sentence for a product card, one paragraph for a blog preview, several for a full article layout.

A practical workflow: generate a short block for hero sections, a medium block for feature cards, and a longer block for article previews, then reuse whichever fits instead of inventing new filler each time. Once it's pasted in, check the page on both desktop and mobile — a headline that looks balanced on a wide screen can turn cramped on a phone if the body copy underneath is too dense, and that's exactly the kind of problem placeholder text is supposed to catch early.

When to replace it

Replace Lorem Ipsum the moment real copy is ready, and don't let it linger in a design that's close to shipping — a public page with visible filler text reads as unfinished, even if the layout itself is done. Avoid mixing placeholder styles in the same mockup, too: if one section runs Lorem Ipsum and another runs improvised real sentences, the page reads as inconsistent and gets harder to review.
