Favicon Generator Online: Create a Website Icon in Minutes

📅 2026-06-16⏱ 7 min read✍ ToolsNowPro TeamUpdated 2026-07-23

A favicon is the tiny icon that appears in browser tabs, bookmarks, and search results. It's small, but it makes a site feel finished and easier to recognize at a glance — especially for anyone with a dozen tabs open who's scanning for yours by shape and color rather than reading the title text.

A favicon generator helps you turn a logo or simple mark into the right size and format without fiddling with export settings, which matters most when you want something clean quickly for a new site or side project.

Why favicons matter

Favicons improve recognition: when someone has multiple tabs open, the icon helps them find your site faster than reading text would. It also makes the bookmark bar look more polished and can make a young brand feel more established than it otherwise would.

Even though the icon is small, it still needs to be readable. Simple shapes and bold contrast consistently work better than fine detail, which disappears entirely at tab size.

Turning a logo into a favicon

The fastest workflow starts with a simple logo, letter mark, or symbol — not your full logo lockup with a wordmark, which will just turn into an unreadable smear at 16 pixels. Strip away anything that isn't essential: thin strokes, small text, subtle gradients, and drop shadows all disappear or turn muddy at favicon size, so the version that reads clearly at full size is rarely the version that reads clearly in a tab.

How to make one

  • Start with a simple logo, letter mark, or symbol — remove detail that won't survive shrinking to 16×16.
  • Generate or export it at the right size and format for browser use.
  • Upload the output to your site and test it in an actual browser tab, not just in an image preview.

If the design still feels busy at small size, simplify it further before generating the final file — the best favicons are consistently the simplest ones, not the most detailed.

Good favicon habits before you publish

Use high contrast, check the icon on both a dark and a light browser tab (many people run one or the other, and a favicon that vanishes on a dark tab is a common oversight), and confirm it's still legible at the smallest size a browser will show it — typically 16×16 pixels. A favicon generator is a fast way to test all of that without guessing, since you can preview the shrunk-down result immediately instead of exporting blind.
