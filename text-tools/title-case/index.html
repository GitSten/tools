What title case does

Title case capitalizes the main words of a heading. Short words stay lowercase unless they are the first or last word: a, an, the, and, but, or, nor, for, so, yet, at, by, in, of, on, to, up, as, vs, from, with, into, over, than, via, and per. "a guide to the new api" becomes "A Guide to the New Api".

That last word shows the limit. "Api" is capitalized like any other word. The converter does not know that API is an acronym, and it does not look up the Chicago Manual of Style or AP for exceptions. It follows one consistent short-word list so you can see exactly what changed.

Title case compared with capitalized case

Capitalized case capitalizes every word, including "a" and "the". Title case is the one to use for articles, videos, and chapter titles. Capitalized case fits name lists and places where every word should look equal.

After converting, scan acronyms and names. You may want "API", "iPhone", or "macOS" adjusted by hand. Sentence case is the other common headline style, especially in product design.

Questions

Is the text uploaded?

No. Conversion happens in this browser. The text is not sent to a ToolsNowPro server.

Does the result cost anything?

No. You can copy or download it without an account.

Will proper names stay capitalized?

Only if the format you chose keeps them. Sentence case lowercases the sentence first, so check names afterward.

When should I use Title Case?

Capitalize headline words and leave short words such as a, the, and of lowercase, unless they are first or last.
